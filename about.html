TFW Program Violations Tracker

Database About & context

What this site is

This site republishes the Government of Canada's public list of employers found non-compliant under the Temporary Foreign Worker Program (TFWP) and International Mobility Program (IMP), in a searchable format. Every entry represents a final compliance decision made by Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) after an inspection — not an allegation, and not simply a business that applied for a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA).

What this site is not

A business is not listed here just because it received a positive LMIA. The federal government separately publishes a list of all employers with an approved LMIA (open data portal), but approval alone is not evidence of wrongdoing, and that list is not reproduced here for that reason. This tracker only includes employers the government has already found, through its own inspection process, to have violated program conditions.

Data source & licensing

Data is pulled from the government's own published dataset at canada.ca/.../non_compliant.json, which backs the official page Employers who have been found non-compliant. It is reproduced here in part under Canada.ca's non-commercial reuse terms, with attribution and a link to the original. This site is independently run and has no affiliation with, and is not endorsed by, the Government of Canada.

The underlying government list is updated on an ongoing basis as new compliance decisions are finalized — it is not a fixed quarterly release. This site's snapshot is refreshed periodically by re-running an import script; see the timestamp on the database page for the last update.

Why this exists

Reporting and court records show that abuse of the TFW program is a real, documented problem, not an isolated one:

  • A Calgary restaurant case resulted in jail sentences after workers were charged $8,000–$24,000 in supposed "government fees" against an actual LMIA processing fee of $1,000 (HR Reporter).
  • CBC's investigation found LMIA-linked job offers being resold for upwards of $27,000, despite employers being legally prohibited from charging foreign workers recruitment fees (CBC).
  • ESDC's own compliance review for fiscal 2025–26 found 12% of inspected employers non-compliant, with penalties exceeding $10.2 million and 30 employers banned from the program.

This site's purpose is to make the government's own enforcement record easier to search and browse — not to accuse any specific business of wrongdoing beyond what the government has already formally found and published. If you believe an entry is inaccurate, it should be raised with ESDC/IRCC directly, since this site only mirrors their published data.

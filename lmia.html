What this list is: every row is an employer that applied for, and was approved for, an LMIA. This is the government's full positive-LMIA history (2014–present). Approval alone is not evidence of wrongdoing — most employers here have no compliance issue at all. Entries flagged Also found non-compliant additionally appear on the official violations list; see About & context for what that cross-reference does and doesn't mean, and for data limits (e.g. employers using personal names are excluded from the source data).

What do the program streams mean?
High Wage
The offered wage met or exceeded the province/territory's median hourly wage at the time of application (the bar has been raised since, including a 20%-above-median requirement introduced in November 2024). No cap on how many workers an employer can hire this way.
Low Wage
The offered wage was below the province/territory's median hourly wage. Comes with extra restrictions: a cap on what share of a workplace's staff can be low-wage TFWs (10% for most sectors as of September 2024), a shorter maximum permit length, and outright refusal in cities with high unemployment. Most food service, retail and accommodation LMIAs fall here.
Primary Agriculture
Farm work — crops, livestock, aquaculture — under a separate, long-standing stream with its own rules, outside the general High Wage / Low Wage split.
Global Talent
A fast-tracked stream for specialized, high-skill tech and innovation roles, meant to help Canadian employers hire scarce global talent quickly.
Permanent Resident Only
The position was tied to a candidate already seeking permanent residence, not an open temporary work permit.

An employer can show multiple streams because this field is that employer's full history — every stream they've ever used across all their approved LMIAs, not one fixed label.
